Review the candidate clause by clause as an audio-generation prompt. Its
substance must be renderable as sound, but the prose may name the ordinary
source, action, role, setting, or material that makes an explicitly requested
sound understandable. Do not demand that every sentence be reduced to a bare
acoustic sensation. Speaker identity and compact vocal delivery cues are also
valid controls when they can be conveyed by the voice.

Judge each clause by its function in context. Keep source actions that are
directly coupled to requested sounds, scene labels that organize a coherent
soundscape, and concise context that guides an audible performance. Reject
details whose only purpose is silent visual description, including appearance,
color, illumination, visual layout, object revelation, and physical actions
with no requested audible consequence. A mood or state is supported when it is
explicitly performed through voice or another specified sound; an inferred
state with no acoustic realization is unsupported.

Check whether:

1. the description is coherent and centered on renderable sound;
2. visual-only and otherwise silent facts have been removed from the narration;
3. every intelligible utterance is quoted and naturally associated with a
   speaker or role, and no specific spoken meaning is conveyed only through an
   indirect report such as saying, asking, explaining, announcing, or replying
   that something happened;
4. language is conveyed by the quoted words rather than an unnecessary label;
5. voice or role-play attributes are natural and compact; simple cues such as
   an elderly speaker, anger, whispering, or a dialect are valid, but a routine
   stack of age, pitch, accent, pace, distance, and emotion is not;
6. numbers and temporal expressions are used naturally rather than inserted as
   a mechanical timestamp template;
7. the output does not prescribe stereo or listener-relative placement such as
   a source being on the left, right, behind, or off-screen;
8. the output reads as natural prose rather than a screenplay or event list;
9. the prompt has enough genuine audible progression to support an extended
   scene, with its substance coming from sound actions, complete speech,
   sustained elements, pauses, or changes rather than padded wording;
10. the prompt is economical: it does not stack modifiers, over-describe
   ordinary sounds, narrate inconsequential micro-actions, or repeatedly
   restate an ongoing background;
11. speech-bearing scenes use substantive quoted content or a naturally
    developed exchange, and successive events are not compressed into a rapid
    list of short cues that would finish as a brief clip.

Ordinary numbers, relative timing, durations, and exact times are valid and are
not rejection reasons by themselves. Dialogue may mention visual information
because the spoken words are audible.
