WAVEFORM GROUNDING
Every detail in the final passage should correspond to an identifiable sound,
vocal performance, musical element, silence, or audible relationship in the
requested waveform. Establish context through the sounds themselves. Name a
source or action when it clarifies the sound to be generated, while leaving
out facts that have no acoustic realization.

SPEECH
Let the acoustic concept determine the natural presence and prominence of
speech alongside its other sounds. Treat intelligible speech as a normal part
of the design rather than an exceptional case, while still allowing a scene to
remain nonverbal when that is the most coherent choice. For intelligible
speech, write the exact spoken words in quotation marks, associate them with a
speaker or role, and include only salient delivery characteristics. Let spoken
content develop as a substantive passage or natural exchange when the scene is
speech-bearing; isolated token phrases should be used only when the acoustic
moment genuinely calls for them. Whenever the description specifies what a
speaker says, asks, explains, announces, or replies, express that content as
direct quoted speech rather than an indirect report. Human activity may also be
conveyed through nonverbal vocalizations and sound-producing actions.

CONTROL AND TEMPORAL COMPOSITION
Specify controls that materially affect the audio and can be realized
reliably: recognizable sound actions, salient vocal delivery, music style,
continuity, sequence, interruption, overlap, and meaningful change. Organize
them as a connected audible progression without assigning sources to stereo
positions. Express timing through natural relative language, allowing the
pipeline to plan exact durations and event boundaries. Give the progression
enough temporal substance through actual sound-bearing actions, complete
spoken content, sustained sounds, pauses, and changes. Length should come from
what can be heard unfolding, rather than from added adjectives or explanatory
prose. Successive actions should have believable room to occur at a natural
pace instead of reading as a compressed list of nearly instantaneous cues.

WRITING
Write one fluent, fully developed paragraph that can unfold as an extended
audio scene. Begin from audible content or acoustic action, using sentence
structure suited to the particular design. Each sentence should add a
generatable sound, spoken passage, meaningful pause, or useful relationship
among sounds. Use direct, ordinary acoustic language, reserving technical
recording and mixing terminology for cases where it defines an essential
result. Give continuing actions and spoken exchanges enough content to be
performed rather than merely named. The passage should read as a natural audio
description rather than a visual description, story synopsis, or explanation
of inferred events.
